# Production Knowledge Base: Acid Techno, Hardtekk, Tekkno, Uptempo

Compiled 2026-10-20 for a beginner producer working with an AI agent in code-based tools.

Labels used in this document:
- **[verified]**: confirmed by a cited source.
- **[convention]**: common practice that several sources report but none states as a rule.
- **[uncertain]**: sources conflict or are weak (fan sites, sample-pack listings, AI-generated genre pages).
- **[derived]**: plain arithmetic or engineering inference from the cited facts, with no source of its own.

## 1. Acid techno (the producer's main genre)

### 1.1 Identity and history
- Acid techno is a techno subgenre that grew out of acid house. From the late 1980s into the early 1990s, younger producers applied the "squelching" TB-303 sound of Chicago acid house to harder techno. Its influences include hardcore, German trance and Belgian rave. [verified] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_techno
- **London acid techno**: the Liberator DJs (Chris Knowles a.k.a. Chris Liberator, Julian Sandell, Aaron Northmore) met in 1990 while squatting in Hackney. They threw their first squat party in 1991 and founded **Stay Up Forever (SUF)**. The founding year is given as 1993 by Rinse and Mixmag and as 1994 by RBMA and RA [uncertain on the year]. The sound mixes the energy of early trance with propulsive techno, "squelchy, layered 303s" over "brutal machine funk", and has punk/DIY, dub and industrial roots. It was looser and more uplifting than the austere loop techno of other UK labels.
  - https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/06/london-acid-techno-feature
  - https://www.rinse.fm/shows/stay-up-forever
  - https://mixmag.net/read/new-exhibition-30-years-acid-techno-stay-up-forever-fold-london-1-news
  - https://ra.co/news/76853
- The genre-defining compilation is *It's Not Intelligent…And It's Not From Detroit…But It's F\*\*king 'Avin It* (1997), subtitled "The Sound of London's Acid Techno Underground". [verified] (RBMA, Wikipedia)

### 1.4 Tempo, meter, groove, key
- **BPM**: 130–150 across sources ([Loopmasters Chris Liberator/Sterling Moss pack](https://www.loopmasters.com/products/702), [microgenremusic](https://microgenremusic.com/genres/what-is-acid-techno/)). London/SUF tracks cluster at **140–150**: the reviewed SUF tracks run at 145 and 148, Lochi at 142–150. Mixgraph's "130–140" is an editorial estimate with no measured data ([mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/acid-techno)). **Suggested starting value: 145 BPM.** [convention]
- **Meter**: 4/4, four-on-the-floor kick.
- **Swing**: the 303 line is usually played as straight 16ths. 909 hats can take light swing. Attack Magazine treats 50% as straight and uses 55–60% swing for Detroit-style 909 grooves ([Attack, Detroit beat dissected](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/motor-city-detroit-techno/)). For acid techno, start at 50–54% [convention / uncertain].
- **Key**: no authoritative source gives key statistics. Sample packs commonly ship in A minor ([Loopmasters Chemical Rush](https://www.loopmasters.com/genres/169-Acid-House/products/18858-Chemical-Rush-Acid-Techno)). In practice the 303's tuning knob sets the root, so the "key" is often just wherever the knob sat [uncertain]. See section 7 for theory.

### 1.5 Arrangement [convention]
- **Length**: club/vinyl acid techno typically runs ~5–8 min. For example, "Acperience 1" is 8:57 ([discog](https://www.tursa.franken.de/Hardfloor_discog.html)). No source gives a genre standard.
- **Phrasing**: build the track in 8-bar phrases, and make every change land on a 4-, 8-, 16- or 32-bar boundary ([violetrecording phrase mixing](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-phrase-mix/), [MusicRadar arrangement tips](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/song-arrangement-tips)).
- **DJ intro/outro**: 16–32 bars of kick plus hats/percussion, with no 303 or a filtered-down one ([Lost Stories Academy](https://loststoriesacademy.com/blogs-and-tutorials/what-makes-a-track-dj-friendly)).
- **Energy in acid comes mostly from the filter, not from harmony.** The Wikipedia definition stresses raising resonance, lowering cutoff and programming accent/slide/octave. Roland's guide: "start with the cutoff low, resonance high, and slowly open it up over time" ([Roland Beyond Acid](https://articles.roland.com/beyond-acid-pushing-the-tb-303-into-new-sonic-territory/)). A track can run for 7 minutes on one pattern while the cutoff, env mod, decay and accent knobs move.
- **Starting template** (bars, at 145 BPM ≈ 6:37 for 240 bars) [derived]:

| Section | Bars | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 32 | Kick, closed hats, rumble; 303 enters filtered at bar 17 |
| Build 1 | 32 | Add claps/open hats; slowly open the cutoff |
| Main 1 | 32 | Full 303, accents biting, distortion up |
| Breakdown | 16 | Drop the kick; 303 at high resonance, low cutoff, delay/reverb tails |
| Build 2 | 16 | Snare/clap roll, rising cutoff, white-noise riser |
| Main 2 | 64 | Peak: cutoff wide open, env mod up, add a second 303 or a variation pattern |
| Outro | 32 | Remove the 303, back to DJ-friendly drums |
| Tail | 16 | Kick and hats only |

### 1.6 TB-303 mechanics (implementation spec for code)
**Hardware facts**
- Released 1981, discontinued 1984. One oscillator, **sawtooth or square**. A resonant low-pass filter that does not self-oscillate; Wikipedia calls it 24 dB/oct. Some analyses say it is "commonly called 18 dB/oct but actually 4-pole", so the slope is [uncertain]. 64 patterns, 7 songs. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TB-303
- **Knobs**: Tuning, Cutoff, Resonance, Env Mod, Decay, Accent (plus waveform switch, tempo, volume).

**Envelope and accent behaviour** (Robin Whittle's circuit analysis, https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/303-unique.html) [verified]:
- **VEG** (volume envelope) has a sharp attack and an exponential decay with a fixed, fairly long time. It drives only the VCA.
- **MEG** (main/filter envelope) has a sharp attack and an exponential decay set by the **Decay** knob on normal notes. **On accented notes the Decay knob is bypassed and the MEG uses its shortest decay.** This is the counter-intuitive detail that matters most for emulation.
- **Accent** routes the MEG, scaled by the Accent knob, into the VCA, through an RC network that softens the attack. That is why accented notes are louder. The **Accent Sweep Circuit** also adds a smoothed bump to the filter cutoff, controlled by the second section of the Resonance pot.
- **Successive accents stack**: the 1 µF capacitor doesn't fully discharge between notes, so each consecutive accented note sweeps the filter higher. This gives the classic rising "wow-wow-wow" on runs of accents. Emulate it with a leaky integrator on the accent-to-cutoff path. [verified mechanism; implementation is derived]
- Attack is ~3 ms (https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/).

**Slide**
- A slide holds the gate open and glides the pitch into the next note without retriggering the envelopes ([KVR Open303 thread](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=3866971)). The analog circuit has four control inputs: pitch CV plus gate, accent and slide as digital on/off signals. "The analog circuit can't distinguish between tie and slides really."
- Slide time is **constant regardless of interval**: an octave takes as long as a semitone ([Patreon: patch ideas from studying the TB-303](https://www.patreon.com/posts/patch-ideas-i-tb-41917616), [uncertain, single source]). The Open303 emulator's default `slideTime = 60.0` ms ([Open303 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RobinSchmidt/Open303/master/Source/DSPCode/rosic_Open303.cpp)). **Use ~60 ms, as an RC-style exponential approach, not a linear ramp** [convention from emulator; the exponential shape is derived].
- The **slide flag on step N glides from N into N+1**. Sources disagree on whether the flag sits on the source or the destination note, and hardware and clones differ, so pick one convention in code and document it [uncertain]. Pairing a slide with an octave jump on the next note gives the signature big acid bends ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8644925)).

**Open303 default values** (a useful starting patch, from the source above): cutoff 1000 Hz, env mod 25%, normal decay 1000 ms, accent decay 200 ms, amp decay 1230 ms, slide 60 ms.

**Sequencer data model** [verified via clone docs; the original's details are partly uncertain]
- Patterns are up to 16 steps.
- The original separates **Pitch mode** (the sequence of notes, each with Up/Down octave, Accent and Slide flags) from **Time mode** (each step is Note / Tie / Rest). Pitches advance only on Note steps, so the pitch list and the time list are not 1:1 ([MusicRadar guide](https://www.musicradar.com/news/producers-guide-roland-tb-303), [TB-03 getting started](https://ask.audio/articles/getting-started-with-the-roland-tb03-synth)). This decoupling is why programming a real 303 gives "happy accidents".
- **Recommended code representation** (a flattened per-step form that most clones use):

```
step = { note: 0..12 (C..C'), octave: -1|0|+1, accent: bool, slide: bool, gate: note|tie|rest }
pattern = { steps: step[1..16], length: 1..16, root: MIDI note }
```

- **Gate-length rule**: a normal note's gate closes about half-way through the step. A slide or tie keeps the gate high into the next step [convention from clones; uncertain on the exact ratio].
- **Velocity mapping for DAW/soft synths**: about 90 for normal notes, 117 for accents, with overlapping MIDI notes to trigger slides (Computer Music tutorial via [Everand](https://everand.com/article/449092584/Composition)).
- **Programming tips**:
  - Keep patterns simple and repetitive, with one or two flourishes (MusicRadar).
  - Too many accents flatten the groove (Roland).
  - Set the pattern length to an odd number such as 13 or 15 so it cycles against the 16-step bar for a "woozy" feel ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/guides/essential-guide/how-to-create-a-chicago-style-acid-house-bassline)).
  - Randomise accents and slides, then delete the ones you don't like (MusicTech).

**Distortion after the 303**
- Overdriving the 303 gave the harsher acid of the early 1990s. Wikipedia names Hardfloor's *Acperience* and Interlect 3000's *Volcano* as examples. Hardfloor reputedly ran up to six (modded) 303s at once.
- Dedicated 303 distortion boxes model Boss and ProCo RAT pedals ([DTronics DT-303, Synthtopia](https://synthtopia.com/?p=141213)). Pedal choice is anecdotal, so test by ear [uncertain].
- **Chain** (Attack Magazine acid tutorial, https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/how-to-make-an-acid-house-bassline/):
  1. 303
  2. Saturation/distortion
  3. EQ: low cut ~85 Hz, high boost because "much of the filter movement sits around 500–1000 Hz", mids rolled back
  4. Dotted-eighth delay with a high-pass filter
  5. Short spring-type reverb
- At 135 BPM, keep the 303 decay around 120–180 ms so each sweep finishes before the next kick ([mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/acid-techno), [uncertain, weak source]).

### 1.7 Acid techno drums
- **909 kick**: tune the kick to the track. Add light distortion or compression for punch, and watch for 120–250 Hz mud when you distort ([MusicRadar 909 processing](https://musicradar.com/how-to/tips-for-processing-roland-tr-909-drum-sounds)).
- **Hats**: put open hats on the offbeat between kicks and automate their release and pitch for movement ([MusicRadar Jeff Mills 909](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/program-jeff-mills-style-909-drums-1)). Add 16th closed hats so the groove feels faster.
- **Rumble**: a second, low kick or sub layer for rumble is common in harder or darker techno ([Aulart](https://aulart.com/create-a-909-dark-techno-pattern-2)).
- **Rave elements**: the London sound draws on rave and hardcore, so expect claps, rides, crash builds and occasional breaks. SUF001 used chopped Amen breaks (RBMA).

### 1.8 Free / code-friendly tools
- **JC-303**: a free, open-source TB-303 plugin (a JUCE port of the Open303 engine). VST3/AU/LV2/CLAP on macOS, Windows and Linux, with Devil Fish-inspired mods and an overdrive. It has no sequencer emulation yet. https://www.kvraudio.com/product/jc-303-by-midilab , https://github.com/midilab/jc303
- **Open303 source**: the reference DSP to read or port. https://github.com/RobinSchmidt/Open303
- **Sonic Pi `:tb303` synth**: defaults include `cutoff: 120` (a MIDI-note scale), `cutoff_min: 30`, `res: 0.9`, `wave: 0`, plus `cutoff_attack`/`cutoff_decay` filter-envelope options. This is the only Sonic Pi synth with a filter envelope. It has no native accent or slide; emulate them with per-note `amp`/`cutoff` and `control … note_slide:`. https://in-thread.sonic-pi.net/t/res-option-in-play/8611 [verified defaults; the slide workaround is derived]
- **Strudel (strudel.cc)**: `lpf`/`cutoff`, `lpq`/`resonance` (0–50), `lpenv`, `lpa`/`lpd`/`lps`/`lpr`, `ftype` (0=12 dB, 1=ladder, 2=24 dB), `distort`, `shape`. The effects page shows **no glide/portamento function**. Its pitch envelope (`penv`, `pattack`, `pdecay`…) is the nearest substitute. https://strudel.cc/learn/effects/
- **Acid Lab**: a self-guided browser course that builds a 303-style synth module by module (oscillator, filter, envelope, accent, slide, drive, step sequencer with scale-locked random lines), with a music theory primer. https://acidlab.duckdns.org/ [uncertain provenance: self-hosted, author unknown]

## 2. Hardtekk / Tekk / Tekkno (German scene)

### 2.1 Terminology: the most confused area [uncertain overall]
- **Free tekno / hardtek** (one "k", French and UK free-party music): Spiral Tribe spread it after the UK Criminal Justice Act. 150–185 BPM with a pounding repetitive kick and **less clipping than mainstyle hardcore**. Variants: tribe, tribetek, spiral tekno. Labels: Narkotek, Les Enfants Sages, Epileptik, Mackitek and others. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_tekno , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_(electronic_dance_music_genre)
- **Tekk / Tekkno / Hardtekk** (German, double "k"): Wikipedia says tekk/tekkno is "mainly produced in Germany, often remixing quotes or audio clips", is a commercial part of the scene, and **is not connected to the tekno scene**. The extra "k"s were used to advertise harder sounds. Named artists: Die Gebrüder Brett, Zahni, Nogge, Crotekk, Minupren, Craig Mortalis. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_tekno
- A German sample vendor treats "Tekk" as an informal name for Hardtekk ("also Hardtekk or Tekke"). It puts the style at **150 to over 200 BPM**, with a punchy, mostly distorted, often gated kick and bass as the backbone. It dates the style to Germany in the late 2000s, fusing techno, hardcore and frenchcore. https://www.vipzone-samples.com/was-ist-tekk/
- A fan site says the style comes from East Germany: Leipzig, Dresden and Magdeburg scenes from the mid-1990s, often in abandoned industrial sites or at illegal raves. Typically ~150–200 BPM, heavily distorted bassdrum, minimalist loops, samples from films, games or political speeches. https://www.hardtekk-germany.de/hardtekk-definition/
- **Practical reading**: in German usage, *Tekk ≈ Tekkno ≈ Hardtekk* is one continuum. "Hardtekk" usually means the harder, faster, more clipped end. "Tekkno" also gets used loosely (Electric Callboy even named an album *Tekkno*). No authoritative source draws a BPM line between them [uncertain].
- **The 2025–26 internet "HARDTEKK" wave**: big Spotify playlists are now dominated by short (2–4 min) "<song> HARDTEKK – SLOWED" pop/phonk-style remixes and "-TEKK" titles (e.g. "MOGTEKK"). These blend hardstyle, jumpstyle and remix culture. This is a different, online-native strand from the East German party scene. https://www.technoairlines.com/playlist/hardtekk-2026-%f0%9f%94%a5-top-100-playlist-on-spotify [verified as observation; whether it counts as "real" hardtekk is a scene debate]

### 2.2 Sound
- **Kick**: short, heavily saturated and clipped, often tonal. Some sources call it "gated or strongly distorted and often tonal" (search summaries of RYM and Vipzone). Hardtekk-Germany: "based on a heavily distorted bassdrum".
- **"Tok" kick**: no source found defines a "tok kick". It is probably scene slang for the short, clicky, mid-heavy distorted kick, as opposed to a long hardstyle tail. **[unverified; ask the producer what he means or find an example track]**
- **Bass**: a "scratchy offbeat bass with plenty of swing", per the Belgian Stallion fan essay (https://www.tumblr.com/thebelgianstallion/632782959038136320/wasisthardtekk). The same essay says the scene is live-set heavy: 30-minute live sets on Korg Electribes (nicknamed "Rote Korg" / "Blaue Korg"). It also says the scene largely ignores conventional song structure and mixing/mastering standards, a punk-like attitude [uncertain, fan source]. The notorious "Acer kick" is mentioned there too.
- **UK hardtek kick/bass technique** (Mandidextrous via MusicRadar, https://www.musicradar.com/news/the-breakdown-mandidextrous-2, from the search summary only because the page did not render):
  - Use a higher-pitched, punchier DnB-style kick with less sub, so the bass can carry more sub.
  - Bass: a plucky Serum patch with a short amp decay and a tempo-synced filter chop.
  - Sidechain-duck the bass around the kick (Kickstart 2 / Cableguys).
- **Samples and vocals**: quotes, film dialogue, game sounds, speeches, and remixed chart hits are central.
- **Arrangement**: simple and loop-based, "often looping a few chords or tones endlessly" (hardtekk-germany). A DJ-friendly 16–32-bar intro still helps [convention]. The newer online tekk tracks are short (2–4 min).

## 3. Uptempo hardcore

### 3.1 Identity
- Hardcore overall runs at 160–200+ BPM. The shift from 160–180 to **200+** drove the uptempo style, which gained prominence in the mid-2010s with a rise in artists and events around 2015. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_(electronic_dance_music_genre)
- Uptempo comes from the Netherlands. DJ Partyraiser is credited with sparking it by pitching up hardcore B-sides. The name took hold ~2012, and it reached festival stages ~2015. Range: **~180–220 BPM**, with extreme tracks above that. https://www.hardcultr.com/uptempo/ [secondary source]. Sample packs say 190–210 or 200–210 ([Loopmasters Uptempo Hardcore](https://www.loopmasters.com/genres/113-Hardcore/products/13701-Uptempo-Hardcore)).
- **Beatport quirk**: Beatport lists uptempo tracks at half-time (e.g. 103 and 110 BPM, meaning 206 and 220). Double any Beatport uptempo or frenchcore BPM you read [verified observation; derived interpretation].

### 3.2 Correction: Dr. Peacock and Sefa are frenchcore, not uptempo
- Wikipedia lists Dr. Peacock as **frenchcore** and founder of Peacock Records, Frenchcore Worldwide (2017) and Euphoric Frenchcore Records (2019). Sefa is "his protégé" and also frenchcore: debut album *Leven is Lijden* (2018), co-producer of the first frenchcore-infused Defqon.1 anthem, "One Tribe" (2019, with KELTEK and Phuture Noize). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Peacock , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frenchcore , https://www.technoairlines.com/artist/sefa
- **Frenchcore vs uptempo**:
  - Both sit around 200 BPM, share stages, and DJs mix them (Wikipedia Frenchcore).
  - Frenchcore's defining trait is a **loud, distorted offbeat bassline** (kick on the beat, distorted bass on the "and"). Since the mid-2010s it has used pitched kicks and bass, melody, sometimes 3/4 or 5/8, and vocal chops.
  - Uptempo is **kick-only low end**: an extremely distorted, punchy kick with stripped-back or atonal leads, screeches, and machine-gun snare rolls (hardcultr; [Sound Atlas](https://thesoundatlas.org/discover/uptempo-hardcore), [uncertain, possibly AI-generated page]).
  - The Wikipedia hardcore article lumps Sefa and Dr. Peacock under uptempo. That conflicts with their own articles, so treat it as loose usage.

### 3.4 Uptempo kick design [convention, synthesised from general hardcore/kick sources]
Uptempo-specific tutorials are scarce online, and the good ones are mostly YouTube or paid courses. The principles below are reliable:
- **Time budget** [derived]: at 200 BPM a beat lasts 300 ms; at 150 BPM, 400 ms. The kick, including its tail, must resolve before the next one, so tails are shorter and tighter than in hardstyle.
- **Layering**: split the kick into transient/click (high), body/punch (mid), and tail/rumble (low) ([Soundbridge](https://soundbridge.io/layered-kick-drum)). One hardcore producer's chain: a clean compressed attack, a distorted tail, and a cleaner low bass layer. Simply distorting a 909 gives "muddy, lo-fi kicks with no bass" ([Renoise forum](https://forum.renoise.com/t/vst-in-renoise-for-hardkick-drums/36066)).
- **Order of operations for gabber/hardcore kicks**: pitch before distortion, EQ before clipping, multi-stage clipping, then restore punch ([course ad summary](https://imginn.com/reels/hypopher.music/), [uncertain]).
- **Pitch sweep**: synth kicks for hardcore use a longer, steeper pitch drop than techno kicks. Elektronauts users quote "sweep time 60–80 for hardcore" on a particular synth's scale ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/tips-for-making-fat-kick-drums/10142), [uncertain, device-specific units]).
- **Tuning**: tune the kick tail to the track's root, or to the 5th. In uptempo and hardstyle the tail is the bass, so its pitch is the bassline ("pitching" the kick: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardstyle).
- **Phase**: low frequencies phase-cancel easily when stacked. Check in mono and align layer starts.
- **Tools**: a dedicated kick synth (Sonic Academy Kick 2 is cited as the common choice: https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/making-hard-techno-a-path-to-unique-sound-design), or a sine with a pitch envelope, then a clipper and distortion in code.

## 4. Hardstyle (for contrast) and the difference chart
- Hardstyle runs ~150 BPM today (historically 135–150, now ~150–160). The **kick is long, overdriven and pitched**, so the kick can play the bassline. The **reverse bass** is an offbeat, distorted bass within the same beat, inherited from hard trance (Scot Project), and still used as a pre-drop. **Screeches** are dissonant synth sounds used heavily in rawstyle (from ~2010). Modern hardstyle and hardcore differ mainly in BPM. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardstyle
- Reverse bass is a bassline that "sucks in" just before the next kick, which makes a continuous wall of bass ([EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/what-is-hardstyle/)). An offbeat bass pluck, by contrast, starts on the eighth note after the kick ([Hardstyle fandom wiki](https://hardstyle.fandom.com/wiki/Hardstyle), [uncertain]).
- Hard techno (the current Berlin and festival wave) runs ~150–168 BPM. It uses a short, punchy, saturated kick that stays at roughly the same pitch, percussive loops, almost no melody, and long DJ-friendly loops. Hardstyle, by contrast, builds to a payoff at the drop ([hardcultr](https://www.hardcultr.com/guides/hardstyle-vs-hard-techno-explained/)).

**Differences in one line each**:
- **Hardstyle** (~150): long, pitched, melodic kick plus reverse bass; drop-centric; melodic leads.
- **Hardtekk/Tekk** (~150–200, core ~160–190): short, clipped, often tonal kick plus a scratchy, swung offbeat bass; loop-based and minimal; samples and pop remixes; German, DIY, live-set culture.
- **Tekkno** (German): treated as synonymous with Tekk/Hardtekk, or as the more commercial and remix-oriented end of it. Unrelated to French free tekno despite the name [uncertain].
- **Uptempo** (~200–210+): kick-only low end, extremely distorted; atonal screeches; machine-gun snares; Dutch hardcore lineage.
- **Frenchcore** (~200): kick plus a loud distorted **offbeat** bass; melodic and euphoric in the modern Peacock style.

## 5. Other signature sounds
- **Hoover**: comes from the Roland Alpha Juno "What The" patch (Eric Persing). It uses PWM sawtooth waveforms that insert flat segments into a saw, three oscillators an octave apart, a thick chorus, and a fast LFO on both PWM and chorus. Classic uses: Second Phase "Mentasm" (1991), Human Resource "Dominator" (1991), The Prodigy "Charly (Alley Cat Mix)" (1991). The typical pitch-bend-down-on-attack is not covered by Wikipedia, so treat that part as [convention]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_sound
- **Screech** (raw/uptempo): a dissonant, heavily distorted lead, often with fast pitch modulation and formant/comb filtering [convention; Wikipedia only says "dissonant synth sounds"].
- **Rave stabs**: short minor or 7th-chord stabs (classic "rave chord" samples), placed on offbeats with reverb [convention].
- **Vocal chops**: core to frenchcore ("industrial loops, percussion, vocal chops, rhythmic effects"; Wikipedia Frenchcore) and to tekk (quotes, film/speech clips).
- **Reverse bass**: see section 4. To make one in code [derived]: render a short distorted bass note whose amplitude envelope rises toward the next kick (a reversed decay), placed between kicks, ducked hard at each kick.

## 6. Mixing and mastering basics
- **Kick and bass ownership**:
  - Hard dance: the kick owns the sub ("in hip hop and EDM, the kick usually owns the sub", [Beat Kitchen](https://beatkitchen.io/guides/mixing-masterclass/12-mixing-bass-and-low-end/)).
  - Acid techno: the kick owns the sub, and the 303 lives from ~85 Hz up (high-pass it, per Attack).
  - Tekk/frenchcore offbeat bass: alternates with the kick in time, so sidechain or volume-shape it to zero under each kick.
  - Uptempo and hardstyle: the kick *is* the bass.
  - Most kick-bass clashes are below 100 Hz ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/tutorials/logic-tutorial-getting-the-best-from-kicks-bass)).
- **Sidechain**: duck the bass or 303 on each kick. The 303 can take a 50–100 ms release (mixgraph, weak source). A frequency-dependent sidechain keeps the bass harmonics intact ([Audiotent](https://www.audiotent.com/blogs/production-tips/mixing-kick-and-bass)). In code, a volume envelope locked to the kick grid is cleaner than a compressor [derived].
- **Distortion and mud**: distorted drums build up low-mid mud, so cut around 120–250 Hz (MusicRadar 909).
- **Loudness** [uncertain; vendor sources]:
  - Club masters sit around −4 to −6 LUFS integrated in the drops, and −7.5 to −9 still works in DJ sets ([Mastering The Mix](https://masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/mastering-for-different-mediums-streaming-club-cd-and-vinyl)).
  - Hardstyle engineers advertise "punishing −4 LUFS" masters.
  - Streaming normalises to −14 LUFS (Spotify); keep true peak ≤ −1 dBTP, or −2 dBTP when the master is louder than −14 LUFS.
  - Suggested targets: acid techno −8 to −6 LUFS; hardtekk, uptempo and hardstyle −6 to −4 LUFS.
  - For a beginner: get the arrangement and kick right first, and compare loudness against reference tracks with a LUFS meter.
- **Clipping practice**: in hard dance, a hard or soft clipper on the kick, and often on the master before a limiter, is normal. The kick's distortion is part of the sound. Clip in stages rather than with one huge clipper, and oversample to avoid aliasing [convention, consistent with the multi-stage clipping advice above; oversampling advice is derived].

## 7. Music theory minimum
1. **Notes and octaves**: 12 semitones per octave; an octave doubles the frequency. MIDI note 69 = A4 = 440 Hz, and f = 440·2^((n−69)/12). [standard]
2. **Root and minor scale**: most acid and hard dance is written around one root note in minor. Natural minor = 0 2 3 5 7 8 10.
3. **Phrygian** (0 1 3 5 7 8 10): the flat 2nd, one semitone above the root, gives the dark, "eastern" tension common in acid and hard music. Acid Lab's primer covers "Scales & the acid mood", intervals, and West-versus-East scales (https://acidlab.duckdns.org/). No authoritative source quantifies Phrygian use, so the claim is [convention].
4. **Chromatic passing notes and slides**: 303 lines freely use chromatic neighbours (±1 semitone) and octave jumps. The slide makes "wrong" notes sound intentional.
5. **Tension through timbre, not chords**: in acid the build comes from cutoff, resonance, env mod, decay, accent density and distortion, not from chord changes (section 1.5). In uptempo and hardstyle, tension comes from kick pitch and distortion, risers, and removing the kick.
6. **Rhythm literacy**: 16th-note grid, offbeat ("and"), swing percentage, odd pattern lengths (polymeter: a 13- or 15-step 303 against a 16-step bar), and phrasing in 8, 16 and 32 bars.
7. **Tempo math for code** [derived]: beat ms = 60000/BPM; a 16th = beat/4. At 145 BPM a beat is 413.8 ms and a 16th 103.4 ms. At 175 BPM: 342.9/85.7 ms. At 205 BPM: 292.7/73.2 ms.

## 8. Beginner learning path: first acid techno track (ordered)
1. **Sound basics**: waveforms, filters, envelopes and resonance. Use Ableton **Learning Synths** (free, browser) https://learningsynths.ableton.com/ and **Acid Lab** prologue and lessons 1–6 https://acidlab.duckdns.org/
2. **Rhythm and beats**: the 4/4 kick, offbeat hats, the 16th grid, swing. Use Ableton **Learning Music** (free, browser) https://learningmusic.ableton.com/ and the Attack Magazine *Beat Dissected* series (free) https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/motor-city-detroit-techno/
3. **Pick a code tool and learn its basics**: the Strudel workshop https://strudel.cc/workshop/getting-started/ or the built-in Sonic Pi tutorial https://sonic-pi.net/tutorial.html. Program a 909-style beat at 145 BPM.
4. **The 303 voice**: saw wave, low cutoff, high resonance, env mod and decay. Then add accent (louder, brighter, shorter decay) and slide (60 ms glide, no retrigger). Use Acid Lab lessons 7–8, Sonic Pi `:tb303` or JC-303, and section 1.6 here.
5. **Writing a 16-step acid pattern**: one root, mostly root and octave notes, a few minor or Phrygian notes, 3–5 accents, 2–3 slides, one octave jump. Then try randomising accents and slides, and odd lengths (MusicTech guide).
6. **Performing the filter**: automate cutoff, env mod and decay over 32–64 bars. This is the "song". Read Roland's *Beyond Acid* https://articles.roland.com/beyond-acid-pushing-the-tb-303-into-new-sonic-territory/ and Attack's acid bassline tutorial.
7. **Distortion and effects**: drive after the 303, EQ (low cut ~85 Hz), dotted-eighth delay, short reverb.
8. **Arrangement**: use the 240-bar template in 1.5 and DJ-friendly 32-bar intro and outro.
9. **Kick-303 mix**: sidechain, low cut, mono low end, check against a reference track (e.g. Lochi "London Acid City").
10. **Master and compare**: a LUFS meter, aiming for about −8 to −6 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
11. **Then branch out**: distorted and clipped kicks (hardtekk) → offbeat bass and sidechain (tekk/frenchcore) → layered pitched kicks (uptempo/hardstyle).

## 9. Open questions / gaps to resolve with the producer
- What does the producer mean by a "tok" kick? Ask for an example track.

- Is uptempo really his target, or is it frenchcore (Dr. Peacock and Sefa are frenchcore)? The production differs: kick-only low end versus kick plus offbeat bass.

  - filter slope (18 versus 24 dB/oct)
